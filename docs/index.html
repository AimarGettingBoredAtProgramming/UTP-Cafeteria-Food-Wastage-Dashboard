        Fuzzy inference system (Mamdani)

        A second AI technique (Lecture 8) on top of the decision tree. The tree classifies the day using yes/no inputs. The fuzzy system reasons with the live numbers (rain mm, PM2.5, day demand), so the advice changes smoothly instead of jumping at a threshold.

        1Crisp inputRain mm/h, PM2.5, day demand %
        2FuzzificationDegrees of membership, e.g. rain is Light 0.6 / Heavy 0.4
        3Rule evaluation27 IF–THEN rules, AND = minimum
        4ImplicationEach rule clips its output shape
        5AggregationClipped shapes combined (maximum)
        6DefuzzificationCentroid of the combined shape
        7Crisp outputRisk 0–100 → demand % → portions

        Live data rules

        • Weather: the Open-Meteo hourly forecast for UTP (4.385° N, 100.979° E), up to 16 days ahead. The page fetches it again every 10 minutes, and the prediction uses the weather at the hour you pick on the time slider.
        • Heavy rain: at least 1 mm of rain in that hour, or a rain chance of at least 75%.
        • Haze: PM2.5 of at least 35 µg/m³ in that hour (Malaysian 24-hour standard), from the Open-Meteo air-quality forecast (about 5 days ahead).
        • Meal periods: breakfast 7:00–10:30, lunch 11:00–15:00, tea 15:00–17:00, dinner 17:00–21:00, supper 21:00–23:00. The survey covered lunch and dinner. Other periods use dinner-style rules and are labelled as not surveyed.
        • Calendar: lecture weeks, study break, exams, semester breaks, orientation, blended-learning and "no physical class on campus" weeks, and public holidays come from docs/data/academic_calendar.js. Orientation and no-class weeks count as Holiday / break, and study break counts as Exam week.
        • Beyond the forecast range, or if you're offline, the weather falls back to Clear. You can override any live value by hand.

        Assumptions (editable)

        • Likert answers are scored 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1 (very unlikely → very likely).
        • The normal-weekday baseline is days eaten at the cafeteria per week (capped at 5) ÷ 5 = 0.72.
        • Factors are multiplied together, and demand never goes below 20%.
        • Risk tiers: Low ≥ 80% · Medium ≥ 55% · High below.

        Limitations & ethics

        • Small, skewed sample: 56 students, mostly Year 2 and Village 5 residents.
        • Stated, not observed, behaviour: students reported what they would do.
        • No sales data: the training data is simulated from survey weights.
        • Independence assumption: combined conditions may be over- or under-estimated.
        • Forecasts can be wrong: rain timing in particular is uncertain, so treat the advice as a guide.
        • Ethics: the survey was anonymous and voluntary, with no identifiable data. All outputs are estimates, and the vendor decides.
        • Next step: log real cooked, sold and leftover counts with 1–2 vendors for 2–4 weeks, then retrain on that data.
